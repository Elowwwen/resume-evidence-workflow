# Visible JD Alignment Review (Grooming)

Run after every targeted content revision and before final delivery.

1. Reread the complete original JD. Identify the responsibilities, expected outputs and capabilities that drive this revision.
2. Revisit the evidence index and complete relevant sections. Search for useful material beyond the current resume. Check personal ownership, dates, metric scope and confirmed decisions.
3. Compare V0, the current draft and the JD. Reconstruct the narrative where needed rather than merely substituting keywords. Review selection, emphasis, summary, language, concision and readable space allocation.
4. Repair material issues within existing authorization. Propose any material deletion or merger for user decision. Reuse scoped approvals; a company-specific cut does not become a permanent default.
5. Show a concise table of meaningful changes:

| Before | After | JD requirement addressed | Evidence and reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summarize the actual V0 expression | Show the revised expression | Identify the specific responsibility or capability | Cite the relevant evidence module and explain the change |

Use actual text, not invented examples of what was changed. Group minor copyedits instead of narrating every punctuation change. Include important retained content when its relevance needs explanation, and list unresolved evidence gaps plainly. If no material change was needed, say so with the review basis.

Conclude with a short concrete review result: what was repaired, what remains unresolved, and whether the artifact is ready. Missing evidence is a gap to report, not an automatic reason to block delivery or judge suitability. Make an overall fit or application recommendation only when asked.

When a summary is included, write it for the current JD from verified evidence. For Chinese, retain useful context and optionally use meaningful work-focus labels. For English, use action-led bullets with context integrated; avoid literal translation. Explain specific evidence ambiguities when they affect the result. Keep user-facing language focused on what changed and why.
